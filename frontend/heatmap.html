Each row is a site and each column is a window of consecutive dates. The number is the percentage of time with the Sun above the local horizon, terrain included: lighter means more solar energy available.

Click a cell to see the longest night, the maximum Sun elevation and the Earth’s visibility in that window.

Near the pole the Sun never rises more than a few degrees, because the Moon’s axis is tilted only ~1.5°. Over the year, that small change in height decides whether the Sun peeks over the mountains or stays hidden.

At the equator, by contrast, the Sun rises and sets every lunar day, and the percentage stays close to 50 %.

Sun and Earth positions: NASA JPL DE421 ephemerides, checked against JPL Horizons.

Terrain: LOLA laser altimeter on NASA’s LRO spacecraft, at 80 m per pixel at the south pole and 118 m per pixel on the rest of the Moon.
